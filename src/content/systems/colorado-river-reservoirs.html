The Colorado River system holds about 60 million acre-feet of reservoir storage, and Lake Powell and Lake Mead provide about 85 percent of it, according to the Bureau of Reclamation. Lake Mead, formed by Hoover Dam, is the largest reservoir in the United States, and Lake Powell, formed by Glen Canyon Dam, is the second largest. Downstream of Hoover Dam, Davis Dam forms Lake Mohave and Parker Dam forms Lake Havasu, from which the Metropolitan Water District of Southern California and the Central Arizona Project pump water.

Reclamation coordinates the operation of Lake Powell and Lake Mead. The guidelines in place since 2007 expire during the 2026 operating year, and Reclamation's Final Environmental Impact Statement on operations after 2026 was issued in July 2026. Reclamation also publishes the 24-Month Study, its operation plan for the Colorado River system reservoirs, with a report for each month. The levels on this page come from Reclamation's own daily data, each with its date; Reclamation describes the data as provisional.
